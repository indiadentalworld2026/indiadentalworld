01

The problem we are solving

Dentistry is one of the most fragmented corners of healthcare. Every participant solves the same problems alone, badly, and in isolation from the others.

Patients

No reliable way to judge a dentist. They choose on proximity or a stranger's review, then discover cost and treatment plan only in the chair.

Clinics

Paper files, WhatsApp bookings, a spreadsheet for money, a notebook for lab work. Invisible online. No idea what their practice actually earns per chair.

Dental labs

Work orders arrive by phone and courier slip. Rework, delays and disputes are absorbed silently. Growth means more calls, not more systems.

Product vendors

Reach dentists through field reps and trade fairs. No demand data, no targeting, no way to measure a campaign.

Education bodies

Courses are mandatory but discovery is word-of-mouth. Filling seats costs more than running the course.

These are not five separate problems. They are one problem — the industry has no shared connective tissue — showing up five times. Solve it once, and the value compounds across all five.

02

What we are, in one page

The Platform is a two-part business that only works because both parts are ours.

A demand engine for clinics

Patients search by location and specialty, compare real clinic profiles, and book. Each clinic gets a high-performing web presence built to be found by search engines, AI assistants and maps.

An operating system for clinics

Practice management and records: case histories, treatment plans, prescriptions, invoices, payments, expenses, chairs, doctors, labs and inventory. One login runs the whole practice.

A marketplace for everyone who sells to clinics

Products, laboratory work, courses, jobs, events and used equipment — sold to a verified, addressable audience of practising dentists, inside the software they already use daily.

A shared data layer underneath all three

Anonymised, aggregated signal on procedures, materials, pricing, demand and turnaround — the asset none of the incumbents can assemble.

The strategic sentence

Patient demand is how we win the clinic. Practice management is how we keep the clinic. The marketplace is how we make money from the clinic without charging the clinic more. Each layer pays for the one above it.

What we are deliberately not

  • Not a review directory. Listings without workflow get abandoned. We own the operational record, not just the ad.
  • Not a discount aggregator. We do not compete on driving the cheapest treatment; that destroys clinic trust and clinical quality.
  • Not a clinic chain. We do not own chairs or employ dentists. Capital-light, software margins, no conflict with our customers.
  • Not a general healthcare platform. Dentistry first, deep and complete. Depth is the defence against horizontal players.

Platform architecture, in plain language

Read this top to bottom. The top layer is what the public sees; the bottom layer is what makes it hard to copy.

Layer 1 — Discovery

The patient-facing front door. Location and specialty search, clinic pages that stand on their own as findable web destinations, comparison, and booking. Built so that a clinic's page ranks in search results, is quoted by AI assistants, and appears on maps for “near me” queries.

Layer 2 — Operations

The private workspace of each business. Clinics get practice management and records. Labs get a case pipeline. Vendors get a storefront and campaign tools. Institutes get a course manager. Different faces, one underlying system.

Layer 3 — Commerce & community

Where money and people move between businesses: product orders, lab work orders, course enrolments, job posts, event listings, used equipment sales.

Layer 4 — Intelligence

Everything the first three layers produce, anonymised and aggregated, turned back into benchmarks, forecasts, targeting and recommendations.

Layer 5 — Foundations

Identity and verification, consent and permissions, messaging, payments, audit trail, and regulatory compliance. Shared by every module so nothing has to be rebuilt.

How it works, end to end

The patient journey

  1. Finds usSearches for a dental problem or a dentist nearby; lands on a clinic page or a search results page.
  2. ComparesSees doctors, qualifications, procedures offered, indicative prices, photos, verified patient feedback and real availability.
  3. BooksPicks a slot and submits. No call required.
  4. Gets confirmationClinic confirms, reschedules or declines; the patient is messaged automatically either way.
  5. VisitsConsultation and treatment. The clinic records everything in the patient's file as it happens.
  6. Leaves with everything digitalPrescription, invoice, treatment plan and next appointment are already in the patient's account.
  7. Comes backAutomatic recall for the next phase, or the six-month check-up. This is the retention loop.

The clinic's day

  1. MorningOpens the day view: which chairs, which doctors, which patients, which lab cases are due back.
  2. ChairsideCharts findings, builds a costed treatment plan, gets patient approval on screen.
  3. During treatmentMaterials consumed deduct from stock. A crown case raises a lab work order in two taps.
  4. At checkoutInvoice generated, payment taken, prescription issued, next visit booked.
  5. EveningDay's collection, pending dues, chair utilisation, treatment acceptance rate, low-stock list.
  6. WeeklyReorders stock from vendors, settles lab invoices, reviews doctor-wise performance, posts a job if short-staffed.

The vendor, lab and institute journey

  1. Register and verifyBusiness credentials checked; profile and catalogue published.
  2. Get discoveredAppear inside the clinic's ordering screen at the moment of need, not in a brochure months earlier.
  3. TransactOrders, work orders or enrolments come in through the platform with payment handled.
  4. PromoteRun targeted campaigns to defined segments — endodontists in a city, clinics that buy a competing material, labs with spare capacity.
  5. Measure and repeatSee exactly which segment converted, at what cost, and re-target.

The flywheel

Five reinforcing loops. Each turn makes the next turn cheaper.

Loop 1 — Patients to clinics

More patients searching makes a listing worth more, so more clinics join. More clinics means better coverage, so more patients search.

Loop 2 — Clinics to labs

More clinics raising work orders attracts more labs. More labs means faster turnaround and better prices, which makes the software more valuable to clinics.

Loop 3 — Clinics to vendors

Concentrated, verified purchasing demand pulls vendors in. More vendors means better selection and pricing, which deepens clinic dependence on the platform.

Loop 4 — People to institutes

Dentists and students on the platform make it the cheapest place to fill courses and jobs. Courses and jobs are the reason individual dentists log in even when they are not treating.

Loop 5 — Data to everyone

Every transaction improves matching, benchmarks, forecasting and targeting. Better matching produces more transactions.

Where the flywheel actually starts

Not with patients. Patient demand cannot be manufactured before clinics exist, and a thin directory converts nobody. We start by giving clinics practice management software that is genuinely better than their current paper-and-spreadsheet setup — sold city by city, street by street. Once a critical mass of clinics in one city is running on us, their combined web presence produces patient traffic organically, which is when loop 1 begins turning on its own. Labs and vendors follow the clinics. This ordering is the single most important operational decision in the business.

The economic argument in one line

We acquire a clinic once, at a cost recovered by subscription alone, and then earn from that same clinic for years across bookings, purchases, lab work, courses and payments. Revenue per clinic rises every year without a new sale. That is the number to put in front of investors — not user count.

Technology, explained without jargon

Six tiers. Built as independent services so a failure in commerce never takes down a clinic's records, and so each module can ship on its own schedule.

Interfaces

Patient web and app, clinic desktop and tablet application, dentist mobile app, lab and vendor portals, and a public site built for search performance.

Service tier

Separate services for identity, scheduling, clinical records, billing, inventory, lab orders, commerce, messaging and search. Each owns its own data and speaks through documented interfaces.

Data tier

Transactional database for operations, a search index for discovery, a document store for images and files, and a separate analytics warehouse so reporting never slows down a clinic.

Integrations

Payments, messaging and email, maps, imaging devices and sensors, accounting software, insurance claims, national health identity and records standards, logistics partners.

Intelligence services

Recommendation, demand forecasting, no-show prediction, treatment plan assistance, clinical image assistance, fraud and anomaly detection.

Platform foundations

Cloud infrastructure with regional data residency, encryption everywhere, role-based access, full audit logging, automated backup and disaster recovery, uptime and performance monitoring.

Three non-negotiable engineering principles

  • Offline tolerance. A clinic with patchy internet must still be able to chart, prescribe and bill. Work syncs when the connection returns. Clinics abandon software that fails in the chair.
  • Standards from day one. Clinical data stored to international interoperability and terminology standards, not a proprietary schema. This is what makes hospital integration, insurance and international expansion possible later instead of impossible.
  • Speed as a feature. Every common clinical action in three taps or fewer. Chairside software competes with a paper file, and paper is fast.

The rule we do not break

Identifiable patient data is never sold, never used for advertising, and never leaves the clinic's control without explicit consent. Everything commercial is aggregated and anonymised. Trust, compliance and safety

Health data carries obligations that ordinary software does not. Treating this as a first-class product area, not a legal afterthought, is a competitive advantage when selling to serious clinics and expanding across borders.

Built into the product

  • Verification. Dentist registration numbers, clinic establishment licences, lab and vendor business credentials checked before a profile goes live.
  • Consent. Patients grant and revoke access to their record per clinic, with a visible history of who viewed what and when.
  • Data residency. Records stored in the country where they were created, with region-specific deployments as we expand.
  • Audit trail. Every clinical entry, edit and deletion is logged and attributable, which is also what protects a dentist in a dispute.
  • Prescribing safety. Interaction and allergy checks; controlled substance rules applied per jurisdiction.
  • Content integrity. Reviews tied to verified visits only. Advertising clearly separated from clinical recommendation.

Regimes we design for

India's data protection and telemedicine rules and the national digital health identity and records framework; health information privacy law in the United States; the European data protection regulation and medical device software rules where features become clinical decision support; plus local dental council advertising codes, which vary sharply by country and constrain how clinics may be promoted.

Specific obligations change; this section names the categories to get formal legal review per market before launch, not a legal opinion.

Go to market and sequencing

The order of operations matters more than the feature list. A multi-sided platform that launches every side at once launches nothing.

Phase 1Months 0–9

Win one city's clinics with software.

  • Ship the practice management core: scheduling, records, treatment plans, billing, prescriptions.
  • Sell directly, on the ground, to a dense cluster of clinics in a single city. Free migration of their existing records is the offer.
  • Give every clinic its web presence immediately, even before patient traffic exists — it is the visible proof of value.
  • Success looks like: clinics using the system daily, not clinics signed up.
Phase 2Months 9–18

Turn on demand and the first adjacent side.

  • Launch patient search and booking once the city has enough coverage to give real choice.
  • Bring in local labs — the easiest second side, because the clinics are already sending them work offline.
  • Add inventory, and use it to onboard the first vendors against real, observed demand.
Phase 3Months 18–30

Monetise the ecosystem; replicate the city.

  • Open product commerce and targeted campaigns; this is where revenue per clinic starts compounding.
  • Launch education, events, careers and classifieds to capture the individual dentist, not just the clinic.
  • Run the same city playbook in three to five more cities with a repeatable local team structure.
Phase 4Months 30+

Depth, intelligence and geography.

  • Payments, treatment financing and insurance claims.
  • Intelligence products: benchmarking for clinics, demand data for vendors.
  • Multi-clinic and chain management; international markets with the same clinic-first sequence.

Why this is defensible

Any one module can be copied. The combination compounds, and that is the point.

  • Switching cost. A clinic's entire patient history, billing and lab pipeline lives with us. Migrating is not a software decision, it is a surgery.
  • Cross-side lock-in. A clinic that also buys stock, sends lab work and hires through us has four reasons to stay, not one.
  • Data advantage. Nobody else holds the clinical record and the purchase order for the same procedure. That link cannot be bought.
  • Local density. Patient discovery only works where coverage is dense, so a competitor must win a whole city, not scattered clinics.
  • Regulatory depth. Verification, consent and health data compliance take years to build and are a barrier to casual entrants.
  • Brand on both sides. Patients trust the platform to choose a dentist; dentists trust it to run their practice. Very few companies hold both.
